**The ask**
Bad news lands better with the right tone, and it's easy to either soften it into mush or come across cold when you're writing it yourself under time pressure. Draft it, then have Copilot check the tone before it goes out.

**What you'll use**
- [Draft an Email With Copilot](../Surfaces/Outlook/Draft%20an%20Email%20With%20Copilot.md) — writes the first pass from the situation and tone you describe
- [Coaching by Copilot](../Surfaces/Outlook/Coaching%20by%20Copilot.md) — flags tone and clarity issues before you hit send

**Steps**
1. In Outlook, describe the situation and the tone you want, and ask Copilot to draft the email.
2. Run it through Coaching by Copilot for a read on tone and clarity.
3. Revise based on what the coaching flags.

**Prompts**
```
Draft an email to Marcus that informs him Project Falcon is delayed two
weeks. Make it short and casual in tone.
```
```
Draft a professional apology to this customer about the delivery issue, with
a promised resolution timeline of July 21.
```

**Watch out for**
Both features are in-app Outlook Copilot and get noticeably better with the add-on, since that's what lets them ground the draft in your actual relationship history with the recipient. Basic tier gives you lighter Outlook chat without that grounding.
